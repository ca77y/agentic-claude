# Extend the lead's synchronous-dispatch rule to cover resumes

- [x] Extend the lead's synchronous-dispatch rule to cover resumes #bug 🔺 🆔 collect-sendmessage-resumes-inside-the-leads-turn
  - `lead.md` forbids background dispatch for `Agent` calls and forbids ending a turn to wait. But most of a lead's rounds are not `Agent` calls: the workflow deliberately **resumes** the same coder across qa, acceptance and PR-review rounds (steps 5, 6, 9) and the same writer across spec revisions (step 3), because a resume is the only way to preserve their context. Those are `SendMessage` calls, and `lead.md` documents only their argument shape — nothing about their delivery semantics. `SendMessage` has no synchronous mode: it wakes the agent detached and the resumed agent's report is delivered to the **session**, not into the lead's turn. So the rule covers the smaller half of the loop and the larger half is unspecified.
  - Background: three leads run in parallel (`quality-gate-ci`, `timer-cue-sync`, `native-platform-config`) all stalled repeatedly, each needing an outside nudge to continue. Verbatim: "I've sent the auditor's findings back to the writer to revise the spec. This resumed in the background, so I'll wait for the completion notification"; "The fix instructions have been sent to the coder (resumed in the background). I'll wait for it to finish"; "I'll continue once the coder's comment fix finishes — I'm now polling for completion synchronously instead of ending my turn" (it then ended its turn). From the child side, coders reported "SendMessage to `lead` isn't reachable from this session" every round and correctly fell back to returning their report per `coder.md` — which then surfaced to the session, where the lead could not collect it.
  - `Monitor` is **not** affected — a monitor event does wake the sub-agent that armed it (the `timer-cue-sync` lead woke on its own 5-minute monitor, unprompted). What went wrong there is separate and smaller: it armed `lead.md`'s 5-minute floor for a review that took 6m12s, so it expired a minute short and the lead re-armed rather than concluding. A `run_in_background` Bash poll behaves like `SendMessage`, not like `Monitor` — its completion surfaces to the session — and a foreground Bash poll is auto-backgrounded once it passes the tool timeout, which a lead hit by leaving the default 2 minutes.
  - A stalled lead is indistinguishable from a working one, and that has its own cost: an outside observer diagnosing a stall told a lead to re-dispatch its writer, the original writer was still alive, and two writers edited the same spec concurrently — they reconciled by luck.
  - Scope: `plugins/ca77y-engineering/agents/lead.md`, dispatch rules and the PR-review loop.
  - Acceptance criteria:
  - `lead.md` states that a `SendMessage` resume is asynchronous and its result is delivered to the session, never into the caller's turn.
  - Every `SendMessage` is followed **in the same turn** by a blocking collection on that same agentId — `TaskOutput` with `block: true` and a generous timeout, re-issued if it expires — with a note inline that `TaskOutput`'s deprecation notice targets fresh `Agent` dispatches, where the tool result already is the report, and so does not apply to a resume, which has no such result.
  - The PR-review loop stays on `Monitor` — it is the right tool for an unbounded external wait and it does wake the lead — but step 1's 5-minute floor is replaced by a genuinely long timeout, since a review routinely outlasts 5 minutes.
  - The definition warns off the two mechanisms that do not wake a sub-agent here: a `run_in_background` Bash poll (surfaces to the session), and a foreground poll left on the default 2-minute tool timeout (set it explicitly, up to 600000 ms).
  - A re-dispatch guard: verify on disk (`git -C <worktree> status --short`) before replacing an agent that merely seems lost, because re-dispatching a slow child yields two agents racing on the same files.
  - The failure mode is named explicitly — the leads described the same act in three different ways without recognising it as one — while not over-generalising: `Monitor` genuinely does wake them, and only the resume path is broken.
  - Cross-links [`probe-dispatched-children-for-silent-completion`](probe-dispatched-children-for-silent-completion.md), the same "silence is not a signal" hazard on the fan-out path.
